Core gameplay

Inside the implementation

Inside Auto’s decision-making

Auto makes connected decisions about where to go, how to get there and what to shoot. HumanController and AutoController implement the same PlayerController interface and return a ControlFrame containing movement, aim and fire requests. Player applies those requests through the shared collision, health, weapon and cooldown rules.

  1. ObserveRead enemies, terrain, drops and spawns
  2. Choose goalEscape pressure before chasing loot
  3. RouteFind a risk-aware A* path
  4. PredictCheck movement and intercept aim

01 / Choose a useful destination

A collision-free step is not enough if it leads towards a corner. A bounded flood fill identifies connected space around terrain and observed enemy threats. Destination scoring considers room to manoeuvre, firing distance and pickup value; retreat takes priority when the current position is confined or exposed.

Known spawn markers have a 48-pixel exclusion radius plus arrival tolerance, preventing a collected drop from becoming an idle goal. Auto reads visible markers rather than future spawn outcomes. If enemies block every exit, a terrain-only route can identify a vulnerable blocker to shoot while movement checks remain active.

02 / Route through a standalone C++ A* library

A small GDExtension adapter converts Godot data into engine-independent grid inputs. The search returns the path, explored cells, cost and status. A binary min-heap orders candidates by estimated total cost, then heuristic cost and insertion order, making repeated searches reproducible.

Movement costs include nonnegative risk. Four-way search uses Manhattan distance; eight-way search uses octile distance and rejects diagonals that cut blocked corners. An expansion limit distinguishes budget exhaustion from invalid input or an unreachable goal. Route costs were checked against an independent Dijkstra implementation on 100 weighted grids.

03 / Predict whether the next move stays safe

The route direction is checked first. Near danger, local steering compares 16 directions at full and half speed, stopping, and relevant previous or preferred directions. Enemy velocity is measured relative to the proposed player velocity; closest approach predicts separation against collision radii and a safety margin. Wall sweeps reject blocked movement.

Safe candidates take priority, then progress and clearance. If all predict contact, an emergency choice compares time to contact and final clearance and is recorded as unsafe. Forecasts cover about 0.65 seconds normally and 1 second during escape. At the last waypoint, prediction includes arrival followed by stopping.

Inspect the closest-approach calculationGDScript

Annotated source excerpt: scripts/ai/local_steering.gd · closest_distance().

static func closest_distance(offset: Vector2,
        relative_velocity: Vector2, duration: float) -> float:
    # With negligible relative motion, use the current separation.
    var time := 0.0
    if relative_velocity.length_squared() > 0.000001:
        # Minimise squared separation over the prediction horizon.
        # Clamp the result so we examine neither the past nor a later time.
        time = clampf(-offset.dot(relative_velocity)
            / relative_velocity.length_squared(), 0.0, duration)
    # Return the predicted separation at that closest point in time.
    return (offset + relative_velocity * time).length()

04 / Connect aiming to the escape plan

CombatAim uses enemy velocity, bullet speed, muzzle position and bullet lifetime to find the earliest valid intercept. If no intercept is available, it aims directly at the enemy. A physics ray checks the full line from player to aim point, including the space before the muzzle.

During escape, Auto can prioritise a vulnerable enemy blocking the route. It delays killing a nearby bomber until there is enough separation from the explosion. Shooting and movement therefore serve the same objective, while Player still enforces the normal weapon cooldown.

05 / Stabilise, inspect and reproduce

An evasion direction is retained briefly to reduce rapid reversals, but passes another safety check before each move. Progress detection catches requested movement that produces no displacement. Connectivity results are cached only while terrain, threat coverage and the starting region remain unchanged.

AutoDecision records the objective, route age and status, reachable space, target, firing choice and predicted clearance. The inspector reads that actual record, with route, intercept, hazard and spawn overlays. Pausing and stepping one physics tick connects a visible decision to its outcome instead of producing a separate explanation that could disagree with the controller.

A closer look

A safe step can still lead into a trap.

The challenge

Auto could wait at a spawn after collecting the last drop, or make locally safe moves that gradually trapped it in a corner.

How I solved it

Separated immediate safety from destination quality. Added spawn exclusion zones, reachable-space checks, escape priorities, and checks for requested movement without progress.

What changed

In matched eight-second fixtures, spawn-lane time fell from 206 to 19 ticks and the corner-pincer case avoided damage. A fully closed enemy ring still caused a hit.
