Core gameplay

Arrange teams of up to four monsters and step through a battle or watch it play automatically. Only each team’s leading monster acts; team order and contrasting abilities shape the outcome.

Balance by design

Designing a readable combat cycle

I treated balance as a set of matchup constraints. A goblin must defeat one troll, a troll one orc, and an orc one goblin. Each must also lose to two opponents of the type it normally defeats. Health, hit count, blocking, reflection and regeneration therefore have to work together across six battles.

01 / Give every stat a tactical purpose

Goblin concentrates damage into three small hits. Troll combines the strongest single hit with regeneration. Orc counters repeated hits by blocking and reflecting damage on every hit. The selected values are simple multiples of five, making the arithmetic visible to a player reading the combat journal.

02 / Derive the advantage from the interaction

For each incoming hit, direct damage is max(0, hit damage − block). Reflection adds a fixed amount of damage back to the attacker; it does not trigger another reflection. Both leading monsters finish their actions and regeneration before deaths are resolved. The following per-turn losses explain the three single-opponent wins.

Goblin → Troll / burst overcomes regeneration

Troll loses: 3 × 20 − 5 = 55 HP / turn

Goblin loses: 25 HP / turn

Win in: ceil(100 / 55) = 2 turns

Goblin survives with 60 − 2 × 25 = 10 HP.

Troll → Orc / a strong hit penetrates the shield

Orc loses: 25 − 10 = 15 HP / turn

Troll loses: 10 attack + 10 reflection − 5 regen = 15 HP / turn

Win in: ceil(75 / 15) = 5 turns

Troll survives with 100 − 5 × 15 = 25 HP.

Orc → Goblin / repeated hits trigger repeated reflection

Orc loses: 3 × (20 − 10) = 30 HP / turn

Goblin loses: 10 attack + 3 × 10 reflection = 40 HP / turn

Win in: ceil(60 / 40) = 2 turns

Orc survives with 75 − 2 × 30 = 15 HP.

03 / An advantage should have a limit

A winning lead keeps its remaining health when the next enemy advances. That attrition prevents a favourable matchup from becoming an unlimited counter. The selected set satisfies all six constraints in either team order; the second monster begins acting on the next turn after a defeat.

04 / Choose a tradeoff, then test its sensitivity

AI-assisted tools evaluated 6,761,664 combinations in a defined parameter grid. I selected readable numbers and shorter battles over the documented alternative’s larger victory margins. Comparing both stat sets under the corrected turn rules isolates the numerical change: the six battles take 22 turns instead of 36, a reduction of about 39%.

For the selected set, all 17 valid changes of one numerical stat by ±1 preserve the six outcomes. Attack count stays fixed, and values outside 0–100 are excluded. This measures local sensitivity around the chosen numbers; it is not a global optimum or a test of simultaneous changes.

05 / Preserve the maths when resolving a turn

Damage is accumulated before health is clamped. A 100-HP troll taking 100 damage and regenerating 5 survives at 5 HP; taking 106 damage resolves to 0 HP. Clamping after every hit would erase that excess damage and change the balance. The model therefore applies the same end-of-turn equation before removing either defeated lead.

Inspect the end-of-turn health equationGDScript

Annotated source excerpt: VisualCombat/scripts/combat.gd · step().

# Both leads have already completed their attacks and regeneration.
for id in leads:
    # Keep the full damage tally, including damage beyond current HP.
    # Apply healing before clamping once to the valid health range.
    units[id].hp = clampi(
        units[id].hp - damage[id] + healing[id],
        0, units[id].max_hp)
# Death checks and queue advancement happen after this resolution.

Core development

The combat model resolves a complete turn and returns events. The interface plays those events through effects, floating numbers, and a journal. Generation tokens prevent delayed animation callbacks from affecting a restarted battle.

A closer look

Resolve the turn before deciding who died.

The challenge

Regeneration and early health clamping could make a matchup change when the teams swapped sides, or revive a monster after damage that should have killed it.

How I solved it

Accumulated damage and healing separately, then resolved health once after both leading monsters completed their actions. Defeated units are removed afterwards.

What changed

The six required outcomes pass in either team order. An independent arithmetic model also matches 6,561 ordered four-monster battles.
